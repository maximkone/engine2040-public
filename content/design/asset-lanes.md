One Kaggle P100 session (16 GB, 12 h max) carries SF3D (6) then UniRig (8) in
sequence and still has room for InstantMesh. Kaggle gives 30 GPU hours a week
and resets weekly, so a session is a batch, not a one-off. A phone card is
required before the GPU unlocks.

Local on a 6 GB box: SF3D or TripoSR for the base, and pinocchio for the rig.
UniRig does not fit in 6 GB, and pinocchio is a different rigger, not a
fallback for it.

## The rules

1. A territory clause is about **use**, not money. Publishing a build on itch is
   distribution, so a bar on use in the EU applies even when the game is free
   and nobody buys it. Price is irrelevant; shipping is the act.
2. The fix is not "give up on selling". It is to use licences that carry no
   territory clause: MIT, Apache-2.0, or the Stability Community licence under
   $1M revenue. All three are free and all three ship.
3. A base mesh is never the shipped asset. The chain is: concept image ->
   base mesh -> our Blender pass (decimate to 3k, retopo, palette paint, one
   512 atlas, four LODs) -> our cook -> provenance row. The shipped mesh is
   ours.
4. No rig comes from any of these models. The free rig lane on this box is
   forge's pinocchio (local CPU, fixed-22 template); UniRig needs 8 GB and is
   for the bigger box.
5. Every generated byte carries a provenance row: lane, licence, weights path,
   input SHA-256, output SHA-256, and the date. Bytes with no row never ship.

## The honest ceiling

Single image in, dense triangles out, no game topology, a hallucinated back
side, and a base that is a prop, not a hero. That is exactly what a blockout
should be, which is why the 30 m silhouette test runs before any of it is
textured.
