# Hunt 5: the Rome game, the world, and sim scale (agent-5-rome-world)

Date: 2026-10-21. Scope: the Roman legionary simulator (`docs/plan/rome.md`,
`docs/plan/rome-codex.md`, `docs/SPRINT-28-rome.md` rows ROM/LEG/MK/CDX/KIT), plus the
two open engine gaps that block it: **C11** (a data-driven game layer: one `game.rules`
file per mode with regions, triggers, objectives, Rhai hooks, run inside the tick) and
**E21** (a job pool in the sim: fighter step and islands through `fork_join_on`, golden
equal at 1 and 8 workers).

Read-only hunt. No cargo build, no cargo test, no edit under `crates/`. Every donor
file named below was fetched and read through the authenticated `gh` API, not guessed
from a description. Method and helpers:

```
node docs/research/hunt-2026-10-21/ghs.mjs "<query>" <per_page> stars
node docs/research/hunt-2026-10-21/ghf.mjs <owner/repo> meta|tree "<regex>"|file <path>
```

Cross-checked against `docs/research/steals-2026-10-11|-12|-18|-20/` and
`hunt-2026-10-21/donors-seen.txt`, and against the two other hunters' files in this
folder (`06-outside-hunt.md`, `07-codex-vegetius-hyginus.md`).

## 0. What our tree already owns (so it is not proposed again)

Checked with Grep before every row.

- `crates/ai/src/legion.rs` **has** the formation enum (`Line`, `Column`, `Wedge`,
  `ShieldWall`), `formation_slot()`, `MAX_SOLDIERS = 512`, `SQUAD_SIZE = 8`,
  `BRACE_RANGE_CELLS`, `think_period()`. LEG3's job is a **caller**, not a new module.
- `crates/ecs/src/parallel.rs` **has** `fork_join_on(pool, range, chunk, f, init, reduce)`
  and a `pool.rs` with a **loom** model (`--cfg loom`, `loom_pool`). E21's *pool and
  reduction* are already landed; what is open is moving the **fighter step and the
  islands** onto it.
- `crates/arena/src/fork.rs` **already** rides the shared pool
  (`engine_ecs::pool::shared().scope`) and already uses `fork_join_on` for the
  per-fighter recorder rows. So the "forks spawn a thread per branch" half of E21 in
  `finish-line.md` is stale.
- `crates/rules/src/` **has** the Rhai host (`host.rs`), the script vet
  (`script.rs`, integer-only AST checks, `script_hash`), the `Rules::tick` path and a
  `packs/rules.rs` endpoint pack. C11 is missing the *data file* (regions, triggers,
  objectives) and the *tick action* loop, not the scripting engine.
- `crates/render/src/shaders/crowd_vat.wgsl` + `crates/render/src/crowd_inst.rs`
  **have** the instanced VAT crowd with a far-row impostor branch. A 20k crowd is a
  *steering* problem, not a *draw* problem, for us.
- `crates/world/src/wfc.rs` **has** a WFC table + bake with an `Adjacency` bitmask
  validator. A Roman camp generator does not need a new WFC; it needs a **groma**.
- `crates/nav/src/flow.rs` **has** the flow field (`bake_flow`, `follow`, `flow_hash`).
- There is no `crates/rome` yet and no `content/rome/` yet. ROM2 creates both.

## 2. Wild ideas (make the Rome game real)

1. **The groma is the camp generator, and it is the whole game in one function.**
   PHG 54-55 is an *algorithm*: from the praetorian gate, strike the *clavicula* (a
   chord struck from the gate hinge with the circinus open) to find the camp's centre;
   circle the corners of the cohort lines (60 ft wide) to fix the sides; then the
   *tertiatio* (a third of the ground the camp needs) with the tenth gate on the high
   point. That is a **layout procedure with a survey instrument**, and it turns "dig a
   camp" from a rectangle into a *measurement*. Our `crates/rome/src/camp.rs` would
   take a height grid, a gate bearing and a threat level and return a **metated** plan
   whose correctness is checkable: the *clavicula* must bisect the camp, the gate
   spacing must be a whole number of *actae*, and the frontage must be the cohort
   frontage (PHG 2: 30 ft per cohort over 720 ft of depth). A player who lays the camp
   with the groma gets it right; a player who eyeballs it gets a camp whose ditch does
   not meet its rampart. **The first camp is the tutorial.**

2. **A shield wall that actually holds: the cohesion of a line is a measurement, never
   a number.** Row 6's `weave.rs` says force transmission is not in the body pushes - it
   is in the *bonds* between neighbours, and CRUSH is read off the compression of those
   bonds. So: give every man a **slot** (row 1's table), let the *bond* between two
   adjacent slots carry a real force, and the shield line stops being a set of
   independent fighters and becomes a **structure** that can be pushed as a whole. A
   testudo is then not a special case: it is the same bond structure with the bonds
   turned upward. And the line's **failure** is a bond breaking, not an HP threshold -
   which is the difference between "the line broke" and "the man at the fifth shield
   had no support".

3. **The codex pipeline: a primary source becomes a hashed table, or it does not
   exist.** Rows 2 + 3 + 17 compose into one machine: the Latin Library's numbered
   plain text is the *carrier*, the CTS scheme reader is the *splitter*, the
   confidence manifest is the *gate*, and the `ref` is the *address*. The rule that
   makes it real: **store the passage's text hash beside the ref**. Then a re-numbered
   edition, a corrected transcription or a substituted text **fails the lint** instead
   of silently changing a ditch from 5 ft to 4 ft. Our `content/rome/*.toml` would
   then be verifiable by a stranger with a copy of the source, which is the real claim
   of `rome.md` 6 ("realism is checkable, not a vibe").

4. **The 20k crowd that reacts to your shield line.** The chapter VI Colosseum is
   50,000 people, and the *reaction* is the point: a man goes down in the arena and
   the cavea **roars**, and the roar is a *density wave* through a coupled crowd model.
   Rows 4 + 12 + 19 give the pieces: the binned density and jam exclusion (the model),
   the counting-sort spatial hash on the GPU (the scale), the measured 71 % familiar-exit
   bias and the measured specific flow (the honesty). Add one term and it becomes
   real: **the crowd's reaction is a function of what the *near* fighters do**, and the
   near ranks are our full fighters. So a kill in the arena is not a sound effect: it
   is a **density change in 50,000 agents** that propagates outward at the speed of
   sound in a packed building, and the *speech* of the crowd (cheering, groaning,
   the silence) is read out of the model, not scripted. The whole thing stays
   outside the golden: it is presentation, and the hash must not see it.

5. **The 71 % bias is a game mechanic, not a footnote.** A crowd that always takes
   the exit it came in by is a *documented human behaviour* (row 4). Put it in the
   fortress: the player opens a gate and the crowd **ignores the new one**, because
   the men inside came in through the old one. A gate the player opens to save his
   own men does not fill with the fleeing because the fleeing came in through a
   different gate. That is a real, cited, slightly embarrassing human behaviour used
   as a level design tool, and it is exactly the kind of thing this game is for.

6. **A Roman road is a route with a *certainty*, and the certainty is drawn.** Row 5
   gives each road leg the Itiner-e mix (certain / conjectured / hypothetical /
   off-road). Draw a march route as a **solid line where the road is known and a
   dashed one where it is conjectured**, and the map itself carries the certainty
   tier that our `kit.toml` carries for kit. Then the player can *see* which part of
   the frontier is guesswork - and the frontier of chapter II is the German Limes,
   where the archaeology is good in some stretches and thin in others (row 10's
   named segments). A map that shows its own uncertainty is a new kind of game
   artefact, and it comes free from the licence-clean data.

7. **Every Roman number in the game carries its own tier, and the HUD shows it.**
   Row 4's registry makes it structural: a number that has no tier cannot be
   rendered. So the HUD reads `pila 2.0 m (Vegetius 2.15)` with a tier chip, and the
   player learns which numbers are solid. The *contested* ones become the interest:
   the Roman foot is 295.7 mm or 297.4 mm, and the game must pick one - so the game
   **shows the disagreement** and the plan already says the table should name the
   choice in one place. A game whose numbers argue with each other in the open is
   the one feature no competitor has.

8. **The camp is a *scheduled* build, not a scripted animation.** Row 1's blueprint
   idea (ordered steps, each skipped if it is already built or unaffordable, degrading
   into a partial camp) plus our own `crates/world/src/process.rs` gives a camp that
   **degrades**: dig the ditch, but the rampart is short because four men were called
   to the line, and the perimeter is **not closed** - so the enemy can enter there.
   A camp is a **resource allocation problem with a shape**, and the shape is the
   score. The plan says "the camp is done when the perimeter closes" (LEG7): make
   the partial camp playable, not just visible.

9. **A Roman fortress built from real Pompeii geometry.** Row 10 gives the Pompeii
   building and wall polygons with areas and lengths. The fortress of chapter I is
   then assembled from **real insula shapes and real wall runs**, not boxes, and the
   player can build the *wrong* thing: too narrow a street, a wall segment that does
   not meet its neighbour, a gate that opens into a dead end. A Roman camp's
   *geometry* is the discipline, so the geometry must be real.

10. **One Roman foot, chosen once, named once, and printed.** The plan notes the
    foot is contested (295.7 vs 297.4 mm) and that our 296 mm is a choice. Row 13
    (a Roman numeral type) plus a `roman_foot_mm` constant in `crates/rome` gives
    the game a **house style**: every length in the game is an integer number of
    feet converted at one place, and the conversion is printed on the codex card
    next to the passage that says the number in feet. When a player asks "how big is
    that ditch", the answer is "5 feet, or 1480 mm, and here is the Latin".

## 4. Verdict

**The single best steal in my domain is `djeada/Standard-of-Iron` (MIT): its
`assets/data/formations/` tree turns the Roman line from hard-coded geometry into
data, and the tree also carries the three-layer map/mission/campaign split with
triggers and objectives that C11 needs, the blueprint step list that LEG7's camp
needs, and the "a JSON string has no effect unless code recognises it" rule that
CDX6 needs.** It is the only donor here that is a *Roman* game, MIT-licensed, alive
as of 2026-10-18, and structured the way our plan wants the Rome game structured -
so it moves the most rows (LEG3, LEG7, LEG12, LEG16, C11, CDX6) with the least
new code, and its numbers are the part we must replace with cited history.
