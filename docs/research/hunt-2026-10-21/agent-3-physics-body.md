# Agent 3: bodies, physics, destruction, motion (hunt 2026-10-21)

Domain: gaps C10, C14, C15 in `docs/plan/finish-line.md`; board rows RZ1-RZ9, TC*, LF12/LF13, HS2, AM24.
Crates in scope: `crates/physics` (hashed authority), `crates/physfx` (float Rapier, never hashed), `crates/destruction`, `crates/anim`, `crates/combat`, `crates/arena`.

Rule that shapes every row below: the hashed layer is integer and bit-exact on every PC and in replay; the float `physfx` layer may be float. Every proposal says which layer it lands in.

Donor metadata read live on 2026-10-21 with `gh api` (stars, license, pushed_at). Files read with `ghf.mjs file`, never invented.

## What our tree already has (do not propose again)

Grepped `crates/physics/src`, `crates/physfx/src`, `crates/destruction/src`, `crates/anim/src`, `crates/combat/src`, `crates/arena/src`, `crates/endpoint/src`, `crates/game/src` on 2026-10-21:

- `crates/anim/src/motion_match.rs` - integer motion matching v2 (quantized lanes, stable index order).
- `crates/anim/src/ik.rs` - two-bone IK, planted feet. `crates/anim/src/fbik.rs` - full-body IK: reach, look, aim, grip, shield, FABRIK fallback.
- `crates/anim/src/additive.rs` - base + masked upper override + masked additive flinch.
- `crates/anim/src/hit_react.rs` - procedural additive flinch from hit direction and impulse.
- `crates/anim/src/warp_ground.rs` - GASP-class ground feel over the locomotion root.
- `crates/anim/src/pack.rs` - packed clips, u16 quaternion lanes (smallest-three) already in.
- `crates/core/src/det_math.rs` - integer-only sin/cos/exp/atan2, correctly rounded, i128.

So: motion matching, foot IK, additive hit react, ground feel, Verlet cloth, Voronoi fracture, bonds, PD servo, body.toml all exist. The gaps left are the ones the table below points at.

- `sgsellan/fracture-modes` (116, GPL-3.0) - "Breaking Good" fracture modes. Accepted as an **idea** (wild idea 3) but not as a port: GPL-3.0 plus an eigsh / conic-solve / libigl toolchain we cannot run or hash. READ for the idea, no bytes.

## 2. Wild ideas

1. **A body.toml that drives both layers from one file.** `crates/physfx/src/body.rs` already reads name, gore, `muscle_strength`, bone capsules and cut energies, but it has no caller (grep `BodyDesc` finds only its own file). The wild move: split the file into an **authority part** (integer: bone capsule radii in mm, joint limits in milliradians, muscle strength in Q8, cut energies, gore code) that the hashed `crates/physics` reads and hashes, and a **presentation part** (float colour, sound class, decay time) that `physfx` reads. One file, two readers, one hash. No engine has a body description that both the deterministic sim and the float effect layer sign.
2. **A fully integer ragdoll oracle, built from the PD law we already own.** `crates/physics/src/pd.rs` has the scalar PD law with no history. An integer ragdoll = the same law in Q16 with an integer `isqrt` for the magnitude clamp, one bone at a time, no contacts. It would not look like a ragdoll, but it would be a **bit-exact oracle**: the same 17-bone drop gives the same digest on Windows, on wasm and in replay, and it becomes the yardstick that says "the float Rapier ragdoll is 2% off the truth". Nobody has a deterministic ragdoll *reference* to compare a float ragdoll against.
3. **Hash-stable fracture that survives replay, by making the seed part of the prop, not the hit.** Our `FractureCache` keys on the hit position (`key_of`), so the same wall hit twice at two places makes two patterns. The wild move: the prop carries a **fixed site list** (baked once, integer, hashed with the prop), and a hit only chooses *which sites are active* (the ones inside the impact radius) and *which seed stream* each uses. The pattern of the wall is then a property of the wall, and a replay of the same hit gives the same cells because the site list is in the hash. Fracture stops being a per-hit event and becomes a property of the object. (The "Breaking Good" fracture-mode idea - `sgsellan/fracture-modes`, GPL-3.0, 116 stars, `fracture_utility/compute_fracture_modes.py` - is the published version of this: a precomputed per-prop mode table instead of random sites. Read for the idea, not for the code.)
4. **A "two-tier hash" receipt for every physical effect.** Row 2's idea, applied to `physfx`: the receipt prints `physfx: bodies=.. contacts=.. us=..` today. Add a second number: the **authoritative digest** (what the sim decided, integer) and the **solver digest** (what Rapier did, float). A screenshot reviewer then sees at a glance whether a picture change came from a sim decision or from the float layer. That makes float-layer drift a visible, cheap-to-diagnose thing instead of a mystery.
5. **A break-by-force rule in the integer authority layer.** Our bonds break by strain. Row 5's donor breaks a joint on the solver's reaction force. The wild move: keep the authority integer by making the **threshold** the force, but compute the force in the sim from the **contact impulse we already record** (`BoneContact` in `crates/physics/src/ragdoll.rs`, `Impact` in physfx). Then "a wall breaks where the sword hit hardest, not where the mesh was weakest" becomes an integer rule with no float in it, and the float layer only draws the pieces.
6. **A hop-spread strength model that is a graph walk, not a radius.** Row 1's spread is a BFS over the joint graph. The wild move: make the **authority** own the per-bone muscle strength as an integer row, and let a hit reduce it by hops. Then the *same* hit produces the same stagger, the same limp and the same knockdown in the sim and in the picture, with no float servo anywhere. Right now our fight feel lives in float code (`crates/anim/src/hit_react.rs` uses f32) and the sim only knows "pain".
7. **A foot-placement ledger in the authority layer.** Our planted feet are presentation (`crates/anim/src/ik.rs`, `crates/anim/src/warp_ground.rs`). The wild move: the sim records, per fighter, the tick of each foot plant, its world position and the ground normal, in integers. The picture then *replays* the ledger instead of solving IK, and the `foot_slide_cm` metric in the arena receipt becomes a number the sim knows, not a number the picture guesses. Same for the cape: the sim records which body it touched (TC1).
8. **A soft float that keeps f32's range but f32's exactness.** Row 8's `GFloat`: 24-bit fraction, 8-bit exponent, integer ops. Our `crates/core/src/fixed.rs` is Q47.16 - enormous range, 48 bits of fraction, slow. A soft float sits between: `f32` range, `f32` speed, integer-exact bits. The wild part: it makes the **float physfx layer itself hashable**. If `physfx` ran on a soft float, the golden could include the ragdoll pose and the debris - the "hash equal with the physical layer on and off" gate would become "hash equal with the physical layer on", which is a much stronger promise.
9. **Break the physics crate in two: `engine-physics` (hashed) and `engine-physfx` (float), and put the *body description* in a third, `engine-anatomy`.** Our `crates/combat/src/anatomy.rs` already owns the tissue table (`TissueRow`, `AnatomyTable`, `protection_q16`), and `crates/physfx/src/body.rs` owns the bone shapes. Two body descriptions, two crates, no shared owner. Merging them into one `body.toml` with an integer part and a float part is the concrete version of wild idea 1.

## 3. Verdict

**The best steal is `blugart-dev/kickback` (MIT, 28 stars): its per-bone strength table, its per-joint degree limits and its hop-spread strength reduction.** It is the only donor found that turns fight feel into **integer table rows plus a graph walk**, so it lands in the hashed layer, it fills the exact hole in `crates/physics/src/joint_limits.rs` (one cone from joint depth today, a per-joint row tomorrow), it gives `body.toml` real content, and it is small: three GDScript files, no engine to port, numbers we can retune.

Runner-up: `mrdav30/Gravitas`'s two-tier replay hash (Authoritative vs AuthoritativeWithSolverCaches), which costs an S and makes every future "the golden moved" answerable.
