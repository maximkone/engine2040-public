1. **A regressive best-first task planner over task definitions** | Project-Okran@a91ee71789d6:src/ai/planner.rs:8-13 (a node is a task with a target and a place; expanding finds its preconditions that fail now and, for each, the tasks whose completion makes it hold — the repertoire's *solver index*; the highest-scoring node is taken next, a child keeps the lowest score along its chain; a node whose preconditions all hold runs now and its chain to the goal is the plan), :19 `ALWAYS_NEW` (move and run are never dropped as redundant), :21 `NO_SOLUTION` placeholder, :23 `SHARE_AFTER = 3` (nodes with the same task/target/place are deduped), :25 `CYCLE_DEPTH = 50`, :67 `solver_index(rep) -> HashMap<(task, bool), Vec<task>>`, :101 `holds`, :108 `preconditions_hold`, :399 `plan`, :478 `steps` | MIT | our file: `crates/ai` (beside `goalstack.rs`) |
   Our AI picks a goal; it does not build a chain of steps to reach it. Kenshi's answer is small and testable: index every task by the preconditions it satisfies, then search backwards from the goal, dedupe repeated nodes, and cap depth. That gives a fighter "close distance, then circle, then attack" instead of one intent per tick, and it is pure integer/queue code. Map it onto `crates/ai` as an advisory planner whose output is a list of existing orders, never a new actuator. Kill: if a chain longer than ~50 nodes or any planner-driven order moves the golden on the staged fight, keep the planner to a test/app-only policy.
