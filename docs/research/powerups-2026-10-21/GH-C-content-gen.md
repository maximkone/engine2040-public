# GH-C — Procedural content generation for the open world: donors

Lane: content gen (terrain, roads, settlements, props, dungeons, ruins). Written 2026-10-21.
Every donor below was read live today through the GitHub API (`gh api`) with the repo's own
helpers (`docs/research/hunt-2026-10-21/ghf.mjs`, `ghs.mjs`). Nothing here is vendored: every
port is our own Rust, integer, bake-time only, never in the tick and never in the golden.

## Answer

Our world is one 8-bit seeded heightmap with thermal erosion, a Poisson-disk scatter, a WFC
tile bake and a hand-written district map. It has **no terrain hydrology, no roads, no
settlements, no dungeons and no site planner** (OBSERVED 2026-10-21: `git grep -i -E
"hydrolog|flow_accum|\briver\b|droplet|erosion" -- crates/` returns only three near-misses —
`crates/rome/src/geo.rs` names a *data* river segment on the frontier, `crates/render/src/fluids_gpu.rs`
has blood/water VFX droplets, `crates/world/src/cells.rs` has thermal talus erosion in the
cell halo — and no flow accumulation, no river/lake/ocean mask, no hydraulic erosion anywhere;
`crates/world/src/roads.rs`, `city.rs`, `terrain_tiles.rs`, `dressing.rs`, `lots.rs` all
`Test-Path False`). The cheapest fills are three
licence-clean repos whose whole output is a bake-time table: a **Rust terrain library** that
adds droplet erosion, priority-flood depression filling, D8 flow accumulation and
river/lake/ocean masks (`fabio-t/terrain-generator`, Apache-2.0); a **fantasy-map plugin**
whose road network is a terrain cost grid plus A* plus Chaikin smoothing, and whose town is a
ribbon of houses hung along that road (`Obsidian-TTRPG-Community/Town-Forge`, MIT); and a
**TypeScript world-feature library** that plans ruins and city quarters against a read-only
heightfield and emits per-layer scatter exclusions *before* anything is scattered
(`M1sak1-Mei/codex-world-factory`, MIT). Two more fill the dungeon half
(`giraffekey/tatami`, MIT; and the Delaunay→MST dungeon, MIT, which the archive already mined
as BET-AB10, so it is a correction and not a new find), one gives us the tile codec and the
seamless-normal rule for streamed height tiles (`reearth/terrain-codec`, MIT OR Apache-2.0),
one gives us the settlement that reads flat ground before it draws a street
(`williamcwi/GDMC`, Apache-2.0). Every one of them bakes to *our* format: a height tile with
masks, a road graph plus a lot table, a room graph plus a role table, a prop table. Nothing
needs a paid model, a proprietary engine, or a byte of donor code.

## What the tree already owns (so it is not proposed again) — OBSERVED 2026-10-21

- `crates/world/src/terrain.rs`: 8-bit heights, hole bitmask, 3-channel splat weights, 2-octave
  value fBm (`base_height`), thermal talus erosion (`erode`, `TALUS = 10`). Integer only.
- `crates/world/src/dig.rs`: integer millimetre height grid, 0.5 m cells (`CELL_MM = 500`),
  dig/pile conserve volume, `MAX_SLOPE_GRADE_Q = 1000` walkable slope cap.
- `crates/world/src/scatter.rs`: Bridson Poisson-disk points (reimplemented from
  `Kromey/fast_poisson`), bake-time only.
- `crates/world/src/ground/bake.rs`: staged ground bake (base → wear → scatter) with a
  `MAX_GROUND_PROPS = 1024` prop table.
- `crates/world/src/wfc.rs`: WFC tile bake with an adjacency bitmask validator.
- No river, no flow accumulation, no hydraulic erosion, no road graph, no settlement, no
  dungeon, no site planner, no landmark placement anywhere under `crates/` (grep above).
  Near-misses to not re-mine: `crates/rome/src/geo.rs` `SegmentKind::River` is a *named* river
  in the Rome codex data (a frontier segment kind, not a generated water layer);
  `crates/render/src/fluids_gpu.rs` "droplets" are blood and water billboards;
  `crates/world/src/cells.rs` "erosion" is the thermal talus pass in the cell halo.
- Board rows that exist today and this lane feeds: **UC1** (district styles and lots),
  **UC3** (dressing scatter), **UC16** (streamed 16-bit height tiles per cell), **UC18**
  (roads on terrain), **WD13** (city grammar + map import, owns `crates/world/src/city.rs`),
  **WD3** (Rome district map), **WD8** (versioned mesh tiles, ticked), **WD15/PW55** (staged
  ground bake and the WFC solver).
