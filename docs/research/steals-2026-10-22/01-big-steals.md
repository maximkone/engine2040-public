## The ranked big steals

this tree, read 2026-10-22.### S1 — The whole lit-frame post chain, already written in WGSL, with measured ms

Donor: `IgorKonovalov/voxel-engine@578c8d3` (1 star, pushed 2026-10-22, MIT + Apache-2.0).
Read: `crates/vxe-render/src/shaders/atrous.wgsl` (whole), `docs/adrs/0018-...md` (whole),
`docs/plans/0030-...md` (whole). Listed not read: `shaders/temporal.wgsl` (8.7 kB),
`shaders/atrous.wgsl`'s sibling composite, `tests/mirror.rs` (113 kB).

What to take, exactly:

- **A hit pass that writes no colour**: per pixel position, normal, material, plane flag. Every
  later stage (light, upscaler, entities) reads it. Our renderer has no such buffer today.
- **One light sample per pixel**: the lamp by next-event estimation, plus one emissive sample by
  resampled importance sampling over candidates the CPU mirror extracts at upload; ReSTIR reservoir
  reuse as one mode, a plain candidate list as the other; then one shadow ray.
- **A temporal pass that reprojects by hit position** through the previous view (the world does not
  move; the camera and the edits do), accumulates albedo-demodulated irradiance and its moments.
- **The à-trous filter** (Dammertz B3-spline kernel `(0.0625, 0.25, 0.375, 0.25, 0.0625)`, four
  iterations at stride 1, 2, 4, 8): each tap weighs `exp(-plane_distance / SIGMA_PLANE)`,
  `max(n·n', 0)^128` by seven squarings, and `exp(-|l - l'| / (SIGMA_LUM·sqrt(var) + 1e-4))`, where
  `var` is the pixel's luminance variance blurred by a 3×3 Gaussian, and the variance is carried
  with squared weights. Variance comes from the moments `m2 - m1²` once a pixel has 4 frames of
  history, else from the 7×7 neighbourhood under the same edge weights. A miss keeps its input.
  The composite multiplies the albedo back after the filter, so a material edge never blurs.
- **History dies where the world was edited**, not by a gradient test: every pixel whose hit lies
  inside one of this frame's edit boxes, dilated by one voxel. The box comes from an op the engine
  already returns (`EditResult.aabb`). A gradient test (A-SVGF) stays as the other mode for a
  moved lamp.
- **Two histories, not one.** Their own found defect: a single 0.2 blend shimmered on static
  emissive light with a still camera. They split the blend: the emissive part over at least 32
  frames, the lamp's at 0.2, and an emission-scale change raises the emissive blend to 0.2 for 12
  frames. The still vein's mean frame-to-frame step fell from 0.57-1.04 of 255 to 0.16-0.18.
- **The temporal upscaler** (plan 0030): an internal size and an output size; a Halton (2, 3) ×16
  sub-pixel jitter, phase `frame % 16`, `--jitter off` zeroes it; `upscale.wgsl` at output size
  reprojects last frame's output by the hit position, rejects history on a depth, normal or
  material disagreement beyond the temporal thresholds, on an edit box, or on a miss; clips the
  accepted history to the YCoCg box of the 3×3 neighbourhood; blends the jittered sample with a
  Lanczos-2 kernel, alpha 0.1 (1.0 on a reject); then RCAS sharpen (`--sharpen 0..1`, default 0.5,
  0 is the identity). Scale 1.0 with jitter off is byte for byte the old frame.
- **Their measured lines** (own claims, one dev box, RTX 3080 Laptop, 1080p): light 1.97-2.81 ms,
  light + temporal 2.11-2.95 ms against a 6.0 ms budget, à-trous 1.09-1.15 ms against 3.0 ms, at
  the half tier. Full resolution misses (10.5-14.5 ms and 5.0-5.3 ms). Frame targets 308.7 MB full,
  195.4 MB half. Upscale + sharpen target ≤ 1.0 ms.

Lands in: `crates/render` — the gap B7 post chain (prepass, AO, bloom, grade, TAA), the gap B9
TAA registration gate (their Phase 2 "done when" tests are directly reusable: a MAD-vs-native
comparison that must beat a bilinear blit, a carve that must read within 8/255 of native the next
frame, a moving camera that must keep history on ≥ 95% of unchanged pixels), and the dynres half
of the potato gate G9. Port the filters and the reprojection, not the voxel marcher: they read a
hit buffer, so they are geometry-agnostic. **V5 / E M-L**, in slices.

Also steal the doc shape: their ADR has Context (with the cost arithmetic that decided it),
Decision, Consequences split positive and negative, Alternatives considered with the named donors
(DoonEngine, Teardown, NRD ReBLUR/ReLAX, cone tracing), and an Outcome that records what the
implementation changed against the body. Our `docs/plan/*.md` could carry that.
### S2 — A real denoiser as pure wgpu compute, no ONNX, no vendor library

Donor: `gmrdad82/pfx@df66d39` (1 star, pushed 2026-10-21, repo LICENSE plus
`crates/denoise/LICENSE-APACHE`). Read: `crates/denoise/src/lib.rs` (250 of 361 lines).
Listed not read: `setup.rs` (11.9 kB), `net.rs` (6.8 kB), `wgsl.rs` (7.8 kB), `tza.rs`, `tile.rs`,
`examples/compare.rs` (12 kB), `examples/rt_bench.rs`, four `.pfm` fixtures.

What to take:

- **OpenImageDenoise 2.5.1 run as compute shaders in Rust.** `net::build(&tza::parse(WEIGHTS)?, 9)`
  turns one embedded blob (`weights/rt_hdr_alb_nrm.tza`, 1.8 MB, `include_bytes!`) into layers whose
  weights and biases upload as storage buffers. No library, no ONNX runtime, no vendor SDK — the
  same thing we did for the int8 nets, for an image filter.
- **The HDR normalisation is one line**: `max = 0.19265325 * ln(65504.0 + 0.006260261) + 0.99862015`,
  with its reciprocal kept beside it. A denoiser that does not clamp fire and a lamp blows up.
- **A MAC budget per submission**: steps accumulate until `spent + step.macs > 24_000_000_000` and
  then break into a new encoder and submit. One giant compute pass becomes a queue of bounded ones;
  that is how a weak GPU stays responsive. Steal this for every long compute chain we have
  (GTAO, cull, the belt).
- **The tiling plan** (`Plan { tile_w, tile_h, tiles }`, a `Step { pipeline, bindings, groups, macs }`
  list built once per image size) and the three entry points: from CPU arrays, from textures, and
  `denoise_textures_into`, which requires `COPY_DST` on the caller's texture and says so in the
  error.
- **`.pfm` fixtures beside the crate** (`desk.color/albedo/normal/oidn.pfm`) so a CPU run and a GPU
  run are compared on the same bytes, with a `compare` example and a bench example.

Lands in: `crates/render/src/denoise/` for the path-traced reference (FOCUS pillar 1, the 2040-look
RT tier) and for any future 1-spp frame. This is the single biggest look steal in the set: we score
captures today with no denoiser at all. **V5 / E M** (the network and weight parse is the week; the
submission discipline is a day).
### S3 — The door that verifies its own edits: propose, replay, check, accept

Donor: `BeautyfullCastle/rust-ai-native-game-engine@83e0703` (Orrery; 0 stars, pushed 2026-10-22,
no licence file in the tree — idea-only). Read: `crates/orr_mcp/src/report.rs` (whole),
`crates/orr_mcp/src/agents_md.rs` (250 of 363 lines). Listed not read: `tools.rs` (30.5 kB),
`server.rs` (11 kB), `bridge.rs` (6.1 kB), `tests/mcp.rs` (22.6 kB), `orr_editor/src/agent_ui.rs`.

The shape, from their own generated guide and report code:

- `orr apply "<label>" <ops...> --check "<rule>"` stages the change on a private copy, **replays
  the scene without and with it on the same inputs**, runs the checks, and accepts only if all
  pass. A failed check rejects the proposal, prints the report, exits 4, leaves the scene untouched.
- Check grammar: `<metric>.<stat> <cmp> <number>`, where `stat` is `start`, `final`, `min`, `max` or
  `delta` (candidate final minus base final), `cmp` one of six, and `base:` prefixes a rule against
  the unedited run. Plus `no_divergence`, `no_divergence_before <tick>`, `recording_matches`.
  Examples: `lost_bodies.max == 0`, `kinetic_energy.delta <= 10`, `entities.final == 49`.
- Inputs for the replay are a first-class choice: `--bot N --seed S --players P` (scripted,
  deterministic), `--idle N`, `--last-play` (the recording of the person's last session),
  `--replay file`. Limits are read from the host, not hardcoded: `verify.max_ticks`, `tick_rate`,
  `default_players`, `bot_available`.
- The honest line, worth copying into our docs: *"Every scene edit changes the checksums from tick
  0, so `first_divergence` is 0 for a real change. Judge behaviour by metrics and checks, not by
  divergence."* Our gap D19 and D20 both need this; a hash-only verdict tells an agent nothing.
- Metrics are integer or exact fixed-point, sampled during the run, so a check never depends on
  float noise.
- Each token carries capabilities (`read`, `scene_edit`, `approve`, `sim_control`); a refused
  command names the missing one. Exit codes are a table: 0 ok, 1 host error, 2 usage, 3 no host or
  bad token, 4 checks failed.
- One undo history shared by people and agents, each entry tagged `agent:<client>`, with no
  approval step: *"there is no approval step, so finish the job yourself."*
- `report.rs` renders the compact text a model reads first (checks with pass/FAIL and a reason,
  checksum transitions, only the metrics that differ, then `Unchanged: ...`) and keeps the JSON
  beside it. Baseline and candidate are two different reports, on purpose.

Lands in: `crates/endpoint/src/packs/verify.rs` and the build verbs (D19 "verifier output in every
build verb"), `tune.sweep` (D20, which is exactly a `--check` rule over 5 seeds), and the task
suite in D18 (programmatic referees, no LLM judge). **V5 / E M.**
### S4 — The engine writes its own agent guide, pinned to the build it describes

Same donor as S3, `crates/orr_mcp/src/agents_md.rs`. The guide is a **pure function of the running
host**: it calls `rpc.discover` (engine block, capabilities, value format), `registry.types` and
`registry.schema`, and renders markdown. No timestamps, no addresses, no scene content, so the
output is stable and diffable. It opens with the pin line:

> `Pinned to: <name> <version> | ERP <n> | MCP <n> | build id <x> | game <y> | schema fingerprint 0x<16 hex>`

The fingerprint is FNV-1a 64 over the JSON schema, printed as 16 hex digits, and the file tells the
agent what to do when it disagrees: *"trust the host and regenerate the file"*. The committed
`docs/AGENTS.md` is the output for the demo scene, and a test regenerates it
(`ORR_UPDATE_AGENTS=1 cargo test -p orr_mcp --test agents_md`). Per-type sections come from the
registry's own doc strings, and the value format is the host's text, not the author's memory:
numbers come back exact (`0.1` stores as `0.1000061`, 1/65536 steps) and a checksum is a string of
`0x` plus 16 hex digits — never a JSON number.

Lands in: `crates/endpoint/src/resources.rs` (we serve `e2040://door-reference`,
`e2040://crate-map`, `e2040://error-catalog`, `e2040://game-toml-schema` by hand today) and
`skills/START-HERE.md`. A door that generates its own reference cannot drift from the door.
Our W8 (the plan that drifted from the tree) is the same failure in slow motion. **V5 / E S-M.**

## The shape of the class (what changed since the 2026-10-22 surveys)

The surveys already said the AI-native class is crowded with wrappers and empty of engines. This
week's new repos say something else: **the small ones are now building the two things we say are
hard, and nobody is claiming our combination.**

One honest negative for us: `voxel-engine` measures and publishes per-pass milliseconds at 1080p on
a named GPU, in a plan, before the code lands. Our `docs/plan/*` sets budgets but rarely records a
measured line per pass. That is a documentation gap, and it is why their S1 is portable and ours is
not yet.

## Traps

- **Orrery and Axiom have no licence file in the tree.** Owner rule D5 says any donor may be
  ported, so the trap is not legal — it is that these are one-author repos with no external proof.
  Read the shape; write our own bytes; do not take a dependency.
- **pfx** carries a CLA (`CLA.md`, `CLA-SIGNERS.md`, `TRADEMARKS.md`, `NOTICE.md`) and a 1.8 MB
  embedded weight blob. Check the weights are ours to ship before cooking them into `content/`.
- **voxel-engine's** numbers are its own claims on one dev box (RTX 3080 Laptop) with no iGPU or
  baseline reading, and the design is a voxel ray-marcher: the filters are portable, the marcher is
  not our problem.
- **shards-engine is Bevy-based.** D9 says no Bevy in the plan; take the corpus and the nav-tile-as-
  RON format only.
- **AstraWeave is 1 GB** with 35 MB of scratch logs. Do not clone it into the tree.
- **amoris** is Rust + TypeScript + a web editor + `third_party/rquickjs-sys` (a vendored QuickJS
  build). Read the contract and bench format, nothing else.
- **deepengine forks raylib and its kernel is MoonBit.** No Rust to port; only the artifact format.
- **Not read:** Nexforge, dust, Oteryn (`Oteryn/Oteryn-Game` — a multichannel Rust game server, the
  api call died mid-run). Catalogue entries, not donors.
